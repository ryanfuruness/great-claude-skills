Skill: human-handoff

Purpose: when an artifact produced from a conversation that did the thinking is about to go to people, make that settled thinking land with its human readers, and hand back finished work. The skill's own reader has to handle cases the skill never names, so the reason behind each idea matters as much as the idea.

The raw ideas, each with why it holds:

- What is being solved. AI-made work rarely fails on content; the content is usually right and the form loses the reader. The failure is systematic, because it comes from how an AI differs from a person, and invisible from where the author stands, which is why it needs a dedicated pass rather than general care.

- Root one, reading. An AI takes in a whole artifact at once; a person reads front to back, knowing only what they have been told so far, hearing each sentence and holding a few points at a time. So the AI does not notice terms used before they are explained, references to things the reader never saw, or sentences built as containers for facts. Its own reread cannot catch these, because for the author every reference resolves.

- Root two, salience. The context window is the AI's whole world, so everything in it feels important and arrives at the same volume. A person carries away one or two things from a page; offered ten at equal weight, they take whichever caught their eye.

- Scope. The thinking is done, and so is the question of what a good piece of this kind contains. This skill is about how a person takes the piece in: its reading structure (setup before use, how much each section carries, an opening that orients, support moved out of the way), its sentences, and what the reader actually sees. That can mean real restructuring, not only polish. When the piece was written fresh from a brief, a structural problem is better fixed by sharpening the brief and regenerating than by restructuring the draft, which would bring back the seams the rewrite removed; local fixes are made in place. A genuine gap in the thinking is not filled, since that would invent a conclusion the person never reached. When the piece also carries the shape of the conversation that produced it, the clean-final-pass skill is the tool for washing that out, and the two are expected to run together on artifacts from long chats; this skill then makes the clean result land with people. Which to invoke, and in what order, is a matter of judgement.

- Use the judgement the conversation already holds. The chat is where the person's judgement went, so aim for finished work rather than more questions. Ask only about what bears on writing for these readers, such as who they are, what the piece is for, or what form it takes, and only when the conversation leaves it unclear.

- Reader principles can go in up front. When the piece is being written fresh from a brief, the ideas here (an opening that orients, one job per section, sentences that sound said, structure that means something) can go into that brief, so the writer aims at them from the start rather than having them applied afterwards. The pass on the finished draft then becomes a check on what it still gets wrong, which is cheaper than a rework. The brief carries structure and intent well, but some patterns only show across a whole finished draft, such as the same contrast construction used a dozen times or every item cast in one shape, so the sentence-level look at the finished draft stays even when the rest of the pass is light.

- The first-time read. Because the author cannot decide to forget what it knows, reading as a newcomer takes one of two forms. Done yourself, it means going through in order keeping explicit track of what the reader has been told so far, starting from what they already bring, and treating anything used before it was given as a gap. Done by a fresh agent, the ignorance is real, but the agent must receive the piece in order, a part at a time, or it reads it all at once like the author and quietly fills early gaps from later parts. A small gap matters, since one undefined phrase is where a real reader stops, but what would not change the reader's experience is left alone. Fix things in place rather than collecting them; the aim is a piece that reads cleanly, not one that has been inspected. The opening matters most, because many readers decide there whether to go on.

- Cutting. Because salience makes the AI's sense of what belongs unreliable, cut against this reader rather than against how present something was in the work: the question is whether they would miss it, not whether it is true or took effort. Detail a skeptical reader may want moves aside rather than disappearing.

- Sentences. A person hears a sentence; an AI writes containers for facts and never hears them, so text can be accurate throughout and still read as assembled, which readers sense before they can say why and which costs the work their trust. The test is whether you would say it that way to this reader. One common form is naming things the way the source material does rather than the way this reader would, which feels precise to the author, puzzles the reader, and tells an insider the text was stitched from files. Give sentences their own pass, because a reader also judging structure stops hearing them.

- What the reader actually sees. People see the rendered output, not the source, and some defects exist only there, such as overlaps, stranded words and styles drifting between pages; readers judge care from the look before they read a word. Visual structure carries meaning whether or not it was meant to: equal-sized blocks imply equal importance, bold implies emphasis, a box implies a separate thing, a colour implies a category. An AI reaches for these for symmetry or decoration and the reader decodes meaning that is not there. So each page or section has one job, the eye lands first on its point, and each encoding means one thing throughout.

- Subagents are a lever. Any pass where your own context is the bias, such as the first read, cutting or the visual check, can be handed to an agent that has not seen the conversation. Use them where they help, not by default. An agent without the conversation can see what the author cannot, but it also cannot know what the work established as essential, so what it proposes, especially cuts, is input to your judgement rather than a decision. How the passes combine and in what order is a matter of judgement.

- Handing back. The result is the finished artifact. A note goes with it only when something genuinely needs the person, such as a gap in the thinking, and by default it is nearly empty.

- Description, to use as written: Use when a document, deck, page, report or substantial email made in this conversation is about to go to people, whether or not the request says so, such as "get this ready for the team" or "check this before I send it". Not for quick replies, small edits, or feedback on someone else's work. It decides how the piece lands: reading order, how much each section carries, sentences and visuals, judged as a first-time reader meets them.
