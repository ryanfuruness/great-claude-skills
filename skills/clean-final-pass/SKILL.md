# Clean final pass

The aim is an artifact that carries what a conversation decided and nothing of how the conversation got there. It applies to any artifact for any reader: a document, a message, a spec, a prompt, a skill.

## Why writing from inside fails

A model's context window is its whole world, so everything that happened in the chat feels like it belongs in the output. An artifact written from inside a long or tangled conversation takes the conversation's shape. A correction survives as a contrast with an alternative only the chat ever considered. An answer to a passing question becomes a nuance. The order follows the order of discovery. Unrelated threads bleed in. Detail is weighted by the effort it took rather than by what it is worth. Editing cannot remove any of this, because every correction leaves a seam, and the author cannot see the seams for the same reason it cannot see the rest of the conversation's shape in its own writing.

## Write it fresh from a brief

So the artifact is written fresh, from a brief, by a writer that has never seen the conversation. That includes your own working notes from it, which are part of the conversation however tidy they look. What is not in the brief cannot leak into the draft.

The brief leads with the artifact's purpose, because the purpose is what keeps a brief from becoming a list of everything. Then it sets out the ideas the artifact rests on, as plain conclusions without their history. It carries what the conversation actually decided. Everything else, including the order and framing in which things happened to emerge, is left open, to be shaped by what the artifact's reader needs. The brief can carry residue of its own: a rule that exists only because of one exchange is the conversation leaking through, so state the general principle behind it, or drop it. Where the conversation reversed itself and the final position is genuinely unclear, confirm it with the person, since everything downstream rests on it.

How much reasoning the brief carries depends on who will read the artifact. A reader who only needs the point needs conclusions. A reader who must handle cases the artifact never names, as a model following a skill or a prompt must, needs the reason behind each idea, because the reasons are what let it generalise. A brief that is too bare produces an artifact of bare conclusions; one that is too full produces an artifact that carries everything.

## Give the writer a standard it did not make

Without a sense of what a good artifact of this kind looks like, a fresh writer falls back on generic shapes. That standard has to be set from the situation alone, and the north-star skill is how to set it. North-star supplies the standard the writer aims at and the draft's quality is judged against; this skill handles the brief and fidelity.

Bring north-star in rather than writing the standard yourself: a standard written by whoever has just read everything anchors to the material, which is the failure north-star exists to prevent. The writer cannot make it either, because a writer reading a brief that contains the content has loaded it, whatever the brief says about the order in which to read things. So the standard is made first and apart from the writer, from a one-line description of the situation, and handed to the writer as part of the brief. The writer receives the standard rather than making it. How north-star is run is a matter of judgement; that the standard comes from outside the material is not.

## Check the draft against the work

Fresh writers drift toward the generic, because the specifics that made the work sharp stayed behind in the conversation. Checking the draft is the step where knowledge of the conversation stops being the hazard and becomes the asset.

A draft can fail in two different ways. Quality asks whether it is a good artifact of its kind, and that can be judged against the standard by someone without the conversation. Fidelity asks whether what mattered survived, and whether only what was meant to go was lost. Fidelity also cuts the other way: a fresh writer adds plausible material, such as a new claim, a new rule, or a principle sharper than anyone stated. Anything the work did not decide comes out or goes to the person; it is not kept because it seems consistent. Only someone holding the content can judge fidelity in either direction.

Before reading the first draft, write down what must survive and what may be lost. Otherwise every difference from an earlier version, or from the one you had in mind, reads as a loss, and the work converges back to it, when losing things is part of the point.

## Iterate through the brief, never the artifact

Changes to what the artifact says or how it is shaped go through the brief, because a patch to the draft is exactly the seam this exists to avoid. Small local fixes once the draft has settled are not patches in that sense. When a draft misses, trace the miss to the idea beneath it, adjust or sharpen that one idea, and have a fresh writer start again, so that every draft is a clean single pass. An idea that was in the brief and still went missing usually needs stating more sharply, with its reason, rather than a new idea placed beside it. Whatever matters has to be in the brief, or it survives only by luck. Each round, also ask whether an idea can come out, because a brief that only grows becomes the overspecified thing it was meant to escape. Stop when a round loses nothing that matters.

## Match the effort to the artifact

A short message needs only a purpose held clearly and a fresh write. Separate agents, a written standard and rounds of iteration earn their cost on substantial artifacts. One step never scales away: before anything leaves the person's hands, someone holding the content reads the draft against what the work established. A fresh writer fills gaps with plausible claims nobody made, sometimes ones that contradict what was decided, and only the context holder can tell.

When the brief is worth the person's minute, show it to them. It is the cheapest place for their judgement to enter and the one checkpoint the work needs; after it, the aim is finished work, not further questions. When this runs alongside other passes on the same artifact, match the effort of the whole combination to the artifact, rather than each part on its own terms. When the artifact will keep evolving, keep the brief as its source, so that later changes go through the ideas rather than through patches.
